the life of a crinoid, in ten seasons or fewer

You are a crinoid. The name means lily-shaped. You are not a lily. You are an animal, a cousin of the starfish, built out of tens of thousands of small plates of calcite, each one a single crystal.

Your family has been doing this since the Ordovician. The job has not changed. You hold on to something. You open your arms. You wait for the sea to bring dinner.

First, choose when to be born.
Facts are real; the crinoid is composite. Sources: the usual echinoderm literature — Baumiller & Messing on crawling and cidaroid predation, Gahn & Baumiller on the platyceratid snails, Nakano's sea-lily larvae, Crawfordsville and the Burlington Limestone, the Missouri State Capitol, Lindisfarne folklore, and two state legislatures. Some crinoid behaviour is compressed for play; no crinoid gets to choose its geological period.
